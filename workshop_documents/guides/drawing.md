# Drawing: sheets, manuals, patent figures

## A drawing sheet
- `drawing_make` gives SVG, PDF and DXF files and a TechDraw page with the same views, on TechDraw's ISO 5457 template with its title block filled. Ask which projection the person works in: first angle (ISO, Europe) or third angle (ASME, North America). Left out, it follows TechDraw's preference, else inch units mean third angle; the answer says which (`projectionFrom`).
- Paper and scale are chosen together: the largest standard scale (1:1, 2:1, 1:2 …) at which the views, with room for their dimensions, fill no more than about 70% of the sheet, and the smallest paper at that scale. The iso view may get a smaller scale of its own (said under its name). `fill` in the answer is how full the sheet is.
- Only the overall sizes are dimensioned. Holes, slots and fits want dimensions placed in TechDraw by the person, on the page made.
- With FreeCAD's window up, the page opens as a tab before it is exported, because TechDraw only draws the template once a page has been shown. If the export still lacks it, the PDF and SVG come from Workshop's sheet writer (`filesFrom`). Overall sizes TechDraw measures differently from the part are left off and listed under `page.notOnPage`. The dimensions and hole callouts go on the TechDraw page itself, each on its own: one TechDraw cannot take is listed there too, by its view and text (`item`, `why`), and the rest still go on the page. Overall sizes run between ends of drawn edges at the view's extremes, never a circle's or arc's centre (TechDraw keeps those among the view's vertices, and a flat arc's centre lies outside the part); if the extremes measure otherwise, the next pair that measures the size is tried, each checked within 0.05 mm. Holes are matched to the view's circles by radius at the view's scale and by the hole's centre projected about the part's bounding-box centre, where TechDraw centres the view; a callout placed only by size carries a `warning` to check it in the GUI. Every view's lines are worked out before a dimension goes on it and again just before the export (TechDraw draws them on another thread and keeps the old ones readable meanwhile, so a page exported early held only the frame and the dimensions): `page.viewsReadyIn` and `page.exportViewsReadyIn` give the seconds, `page.viewsNotReady` names a view still not drawn after 30 s, and such a page is not exported. The SVG export is checked for the views' lines (`page.viewsInExport`): TechDraw writes a view's visible and hidden lines as a few `<path>` elements of many subpaths each, so the check counts the points drawn (path data's coordinate pairs, polyline points, a fixed count per rect, line, circle and ellipse) beyond the template's own and 40 per dimension, at least one per view edge (`points`, `templatePoints`, `byPoints`); the older count of elements (`byElements`) passes it too. An export with only the frame, title block and dimensions fails both; without them it is exported once more after a further wait, and if they are still missing the SVG and PDF come from Workshop's sheet writer, `filesFrom` saying why. TechDraw exports the PDF and SVG from the scene of the page's tab, and a view's lines are put on that scene only once the tab has been shown and painted; so before exporting, the page is shown, its tab made the active one and painted, each view asked to repaint (no recompute, which would start its lines afresh) and the event loop run (`page.exportScene` lists the steps done; `page.exportSceneAgain` before the second try). On the sheet writer's files a hole callout's leader lands only on a drawn circle of the hole's size (within 2%, a thread's tap drill accepted) where the hole projects; a hole with no such circle in its view (hidden there) is left off the sheet rather than pointed at a slot's end or a bore of another size, and listed under `calloutsOff` and as `calloutOff` in its `holes` row. Each hole callout's text and leaders clear every earlier callout's: texts tested against each other at the page's size (3.5 mm) with a margin, leaders never crossing, a later callout raised, turned to the other side or set at 30 or 60 degrees until it is clear. A callout's text stays near the view that holds the hole (up, then down from it), and its leaders never cross another view or a text: a callout no longer lands by the iso view with its leader across it.
- `file` can be a full path: its folder is made, and the PDF, SVG and DXF share its name without the extension.
- `holes: true` calls out every round hole once per size, with a leader, in a view that sees it end on (front, then right, then top …). FreeCAD's own Holes are called out as they are made: size or thread, THRU or ↧ depth, ⌴ counterbore or ⌵ countersink (`4× ⌀8 THRU ⌴⌀13 ↧8.5`). Holes no drawn view sees end on go in a hole table on the sheet.
- The title block's sample texts (names, numbers) are emptied, never filled with a made-up name. Give `drawnBy` only when the person tells you whose name goes there.
- The title block gives the material by its name, never by its id.
- `scale` is refused when it does not fit; leave it out for the largest standard scale that does.

## An assembly manual
- `drawing_exploded { steps: [{ title, add: [bodies], note }] }`, in build order. Bodies the same shape are counted as one item in the bill of materials.
- Record materials (`part_spec`) and setups first, or the bill has no mass or process for them.

## Patent figures and a draft
- `patent_figures` then `patent_draft`, so the draft uses the same numerals and figures.
- The draft is a starting point for a registered patent attorney or agent. Never present it as ready to file, and never file, send or upload it: that is the person's and counsel's.
- Formal drawing rules change, and an examiner may ask for changes. The figures follow the USPTO's margins and lettering size; say that is all they promise.

## Sections
- `drawing_make { section: "front" }` draws that view cut through the middle (`sectionAt` mm towards the viewer moves the plane): the material in front taken away, the cut faces hatched, titled SECTION A-A, and the cutting line drawn and lettered in a view that sees the plane edge on (top for a front section). The TechDraw page gets TechDraw's own section view.
- Cut through what matters: a bore's axis, a pocket's middle, the thinnest wall. Feature `dims` on that view measure the cut.

## drawing_make in full
<!-- later: when a drawing_make answer, its page or its files need explaining -->
The keys of the answer and the behaviour behind them, moved from drawing_make's tool card (the card keeps the inputs, the traps, the refusals and the main keys).

The answer holds: files { svg, pdf, dxf paths }, filesFrom, paper, scale ("1:2"), projection, projectionFrom, fill, views [{ view, at, size, scale }], dimensions, section { view, label, plane, cutFaces, cutLineIn }, holes [{ view, diameter, count, label, from, features, calloutOff }], holeTable, calloutsOff, titleBlock, techdrawPage (its label). It holds page { views, viewsReadyIn, viewsNotReady, extentDimensions, extents, dimensions, notOnPage [{ view, type, value, item, why }], template, titleBlock, titleBlockEmptied, section | sectionNote, holeTable, viewsInExport, exportScene, exportSceneAgain, settled { cleared, waited, exportMs, turns, turnMs, seen { changed, recomputed }, late, left } }. Or it holds pageNote: why there is no page. It holds timing { sheet, page, export { wait, dxf, scene, svg, check, pdf }, settle, turns, turnMs, total }, note and step. The answer of a changing call can also hold health { ok, verdict, todo } from the shared check.

Layout: the scale is the largest standard scale that fits A4 or A3 with room for the dimensions. The views fill the sheet at most about 70%, on the smallest paper at that scale. The iso view takes its own scale where it has less room. The views stay clear of the title block of the template. Each overall size is dimensioned once, outside the views, 10 mm off the outline.

Exports: SVG, PDF and DXF go to the output folder. They are the page's own TechDraw exports where TechDraw can write them; PDF and SVG need FreeCAD's window. Workshop's sheet writer makes the rest; filesFrom says which.

holes calls out every round hole, grouped by size and by how it is made ("4× ⌀8 THRU ⌴⌀13 ↧8.5"). The callout has a leader in a view that sees the hole end on (front, right, top …). A hole table on the sheet lists the holes that no drawn view sees end on.

titleBlock fills the title block of TechDraw's ISO 5457 template. The sample names and numbers of the template are emptied, and a name is never made up. The date is today, and the scale and tolerance are filled.

section: the cut is square to the view, through the middle, sectionAt mm towards the viewer. The cut faces are hatched. The cutting line is lettered sectionLabel (A), in a view that sees it edge on. Use section for bores, pockets and walls inside.

Dims and hole callouts go on the TechDraw page as TechDraw's own dimensions, each on its own. One that TechDraw cannot take is listed under page.notOnPage by its view and text (item, why). It is never put on at the cost of the others, or of the overall sizes already listed there.

TechDraw measures each overall size between the ends of drawn edges at the extremes of the view. It never uses the centre of a circle or arc, which TechDraw also keeps as a vertex. If the extremes do not measure the size, the next pair that does is tried. A size is kept only within 0.05 mm of the size of the part.

Each hole is found among the circles of the view by its radius at the scale of the view. It is also found by its centre, as the view projects it about the centre of the part. TechDraw centres the part there. A callout whose place matches no circle goes on the nearest circle of that size in the view. A warning then says: check that it is the hole meant.

The lines of every view are worked out before any dimension goes on it, and again before the export. TechDraw does that on another thread. page.viewsReadyIn says how long that took. page.viewsNotReady names a view still not drawn after 30 s. The page is never exported while a view is not drawn. Its SVG is checked to hold the lines of the views (page.viewsInExport). page.viewsInExport holds: drawn elements, the template's, dimensions, the edges of the views, points drawn and the template's. TechDraw writes the lines of a view as a few paths of many subpaths each. So the check also passes on points. The points drawn beyond the template's must be at least one per view edge, plus an allowance per dimension. When the check fails, the page is exported once more after a further wait. When it still fails, neither SVG nor PDF is taken from the page (filesFrom says why).

After the export, nothing on the page stays marked as changed. TechDraw's own exports mark the template of the page (drawing it writes its size back). On views at any scale but 1:1, they also mark its dimensions. The PDF export redraws every view, and a dimension that executes writes its references again. The lines of a redrawn view come back a moment later, on another thread. TechDraw then recomputes the dimensions of that view once more. The call waits for that (page.settled.waited seconds). The wait is at most four times the drawing time of the views, plus two seconds. It is 5 s at least, 30 s at most. It then takes the mark off where only those writes of TechDraw's own made it. page.settled.cleared holds the names of those objects. It recomputes nothing. page.settled.late names dimensions not seen recomputed again in that time. The health of the next call can then still read them as not recomputed yet; a recompute clears it. page.settled.left [{ object, why }] names what stays marked for a reason of its own. That is: marked before the export, or something else of it changed.

timing holds where the time of the call went, in milliseconds. sheet: Workshop's own projection of the views, the layout, and the dimensions and callouts of the files. page: the TechDraw page made, with the wait for the lines of its views (page.viewsReadyIn, seconds). export { wait, dxf, scene, svg, check, pdf }: wait is for the views, before anything is written. scene: the tab of the page shown and painted. check: the SVG read back, once, for the lines of the views and the template. pdf: TechDraw's own export. The PDF export ends by redrawing every view of the page, and the wait afterwards is for that. It cannot be left out of TechDraw's PDF export. settle: the wait after the export (page.settled.waited, in ms). turns and turnMs: how many turns of FreeCAD's event loop the whole call ran, and the time inside them. A turn runs all that the window has waiting, not only TechDraw's work. Many milliseconds a turn means that the window is slow to turn, not the drawing. total: the whole call. page.settled gives the same for the wait alone (turns, turnMs). It also holds exportMs (the export as a whole, as timed round it) and seen { changed, recomputed }. seen holds the property writes and recomputes of the document. They are watched from the start of the export to the end of the wait.

A drawing with a page costs the lines of the views twice, and the exports. The lines are drawn once for the page, and once more after TechDraw's PDF export redraws them. Measured on the Mac on a small part, before the wait after the export: 0.8 s a drawing (run 2). With the wait (run 3): 4.6 and 5.7 s. Of that, the wait was 0.95 and 1.49 s. The first lines of the views took 1.15 and 1.03 s, where before they took 0.20 and 0.24 s. The rest was in no field then; timing is for that. page: false makes the files only, with no TechDraw and no wait.

TechDraw draws the SVG and PDF of the page from the scene of the tab of the page. The lines of its views exist only after the tab is shown and painted. So before the export, the page is shown as a tab, and that tab is made active and painted. Every view is asked to repaint (no recompute), and the event loop runs. page.exportScene lists the steps done (show, activate, paint, redraw, process). page.exportSceneAgain lists those before the second export.

On the files of the sheet writer, a hole callout's leader lands only on a drawn circle. The circle must have the size of the hole (within 2%). The tap drill of a thread is accepted. The circle must be where the hole projects in that view. A hole with no such circle (hidden in that view, or drawn otherwise) is left off the sheet. It is never put on another round edge, such as the end of a slot. It is never put on a bore of another size. It is listed under calloutsOff (view, label, why) and in its holes row (calloutOff).

Hole callouts are kept apart. The text of each one stays clear of the views, the texts and every earlier callout. The text size is the sheet's, and the page's is 3.5 mm, with a margin. Its leaders cross no earlier leader and do not run through an earlier text. A callout goes 6 mm further a try: up, then down, on either side, at 45, 30 or 60 degrees. Its text stays near the view that holds the hole. Its leaders never cross another view or a text on the sheet. The text stays in the drawing area and off the title block.
